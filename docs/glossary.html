Glossary

Every acronym, term of art, and named system across the research library — 17 in all, defined in plain language, each linked to the corpora that use it. Filter below, or jump by letter.

A

ACR automatic content recognition
A feature built into many smart TVs that recognizes what's on screen by sampling the picture, so the set knows what you watched and can target ads to it.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

B

Backstage Yahoo Backstage
Yahoo's platform for packaging and selling its own ad inventory to buyers, including curated audience deals.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

C

clean room clean rooms, data clean room
A secure, neutral space where two companies compare their customer data to find the overlap — without either side handing over its raw list.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread
ClearLine Magnite ClearLine
Magnite's tool that lets advertisers buy its inventory directly, skipping the demand-side platform — the seller reaching straight for the buyer's budget.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread
CTV Connected TV, connected television
Television watched over the internet — a smart TV, a Roku or Fire Stick, an app like Hulu — as opposed to old cable or broadcast.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

D

DSP DSPs, demand-side platform, demand-side platforms
The software an advertiser uses to buy ad space automatically — it decides, in a fraction of a second, which ad to show which viewer and how much to bid.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

F

first-party data
Information a company collects directly from its own customers — what they watched, bought, or signed up for — as opposed to data bought from outsiders.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

H

header bidding
A trick that lets many buyers bid on a web ad slot at the same time before the page loads, instead of asking them one after another — more competition, higher prices for the publisher.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

O

OpenPath The Trade Desk OpenPath, Trade Desk OpenPath
The Trade Desk's direct connection into publishers' ad space — a way for the big buyer to reach inventory without routing through a supply-side platform in between.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread
OpenRTB
The shared rulebook — a technical standard — that lets all these buying and selling systems speak the same language during a real-time ad auction.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

P

PMP private marketplace, private marketplaces
An invitation-only version of the ad auction: a publisher offers select buyers its better inventory at agreed terms — a velvet-rope version of the open market.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread
programmatic
Buying and selling ads by automated auction in real time, machine-to-machine, instead of two people negotiating each deal by hand.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

S

SPO supply path optimization, supply-path optimization
A buyer's effort to reach the same ad through the shortest, cheapest, most trustworthy route — cutting out the redundant middlemen who each take a fee.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread
SSP SSPs, supply-side platform, supply-side platforms
The mirror image of a DSP: the software a publisher (say, a streaming app) uses to sell its ad space, auctioning each slot to the highest bidder.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

T

take rate
The slice of each advertising dollar a middleman keeps as the money passes through — the platform's cut.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

U

Universal Ads Comcast Universal Ads
Comcast's self-serve platform for buying TV and streaming ads across NBCUniversal and partner inventory in one place, aimed especially at smaller advertisers.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread

W

walled garden walled gardens
A giant platform (think Google, Amazon, a big streamer) that keeps its audience data and ad tools inside its own fences — you can advertise there, but on its terms.Appears in Who Keeps the Spread
